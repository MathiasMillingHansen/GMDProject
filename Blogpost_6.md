# Conclusion:

## link to youtube demonstration of game: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vjyP7NZsWk8&ab_channel=MathiasHansen

Even though the final product is not exactly what I initially set out to create, I still believe this project has been a very valuable experience in understanding what it’s really like to develop a game. At the beginning, my goals were far too ambitious for a first-time project, but as I progressed, I learned to adapt and scale down my ideas into something more manageable—something I could realistically finish and feel proud of. In the end, this became my very first completed game, and that in itself is a huge milestone.

To save time and stay focused on learning the basics of development, I chose to use artwork from the Unity Asset Store. Since this was my first attempt at making a game, I wanted to prioritize gameplay, mechanics, and learning the tools over creating custom art. This decision allowed me to spend more time experimenting with Unity and understanding how different systems work together in a game.

Throughout the development process, I discovered that trial and error is a powerful teacher. Rather than immediately searching for solutions online, I tried to solve problems myself first. This approach helped me build a deeper understanding of the Unity engine and improved my problem-solving skills. Of course, when I did need help, I found the Unity community and documentation to be incredibly useful.

The final game is a 2D platformer where the player navigates through a short level, avoids enemies, and collects items. I used Unity’s Tilemap system to build the level layout and added simple collision, movement, and pickup systems. The game also includes basic UI elements like a main menu, pause menu and settings for music and fullscreen. Even though the gameplay is simple, I’m proud of how everything fits together and runs smoothly. Seeing the game go from a blank screen to something playable was incredibly rewarding.

Overall, this project has shown me both the challenges and rewards of game development. I’ve learned how to work within my limits, how to troubleshoot, and how to take an idea and turn it into something real. Even though it isn’t perfect, I now have a better sense of what it takes to make a game, and I’m excited to keep learning and improving.
